The baby will sleep through most of the first Christmas. There will be a photograph on a rug next to a pile of paper, a large box with a bow that gets opened by a parent, and at some point a relative holding a soft toy above the pram saying that this one is for when they are bigger. The baby's contribution to the entire event is a nap.

That is not a reason to skip the present. It just changes who the present is really for.

## The baby does not know, and that is the useful part

A child under one has no concept of a gift, no memory of the day, and no opinion about the wrapping. What they have is a set of physical needs and a very short list of things that genuinely interest them: faces, movement, contrast, and something to grab.

This means a first-Christmas gift can be judged entirely on what the household does with it in January. If it is still in use in March, it was a good present. If it went into a cupboard the same week, it was a photo prop, and a photo prop is a perfectly fine thing to buy as long as you know that is what it is.

## What the parents actually need in December

The first year is the most exhausted year most families will have, and December adds visitors, travel, and a baby who is off their usual routine. Practical gifts do not feel as charming in the moment, but they are the ones that produce a real thank-you.

The categories that consistently work are tied to feeding, sleeping, and cleaning, because those are the things that run out. More of whatever the family already uses is almost never wrong. So is a gift of time: a meal delivered in January, an afternoon of babysitting, or a person who will hold the baby while a parent sleeps.

If you want something that is both useful and specific, [a play mat for tummy time](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=baby+tummy+time+play+mat&tag=cnxixi-20) does a lot of quiet work between months three and nine. It is where tummy time happens, it wipes clean, and it does not need batteries.

## Practical and photographic are different budgets

There is nothing wrong with a keepsake, provided you are honest that it is a keepsake. A hand-stitched name blanket, an ornament, an engraved spoon, or a memory book is a present to the parents, to the household archive, and to a child who will be interested in it at eight years old.

Just do not confuse the two. A personalised object is not a substitute for the thing the family will actually go through, and a household can only absorb so many keepsakes before they become a storage problem. One keepsake plus one useful thing is a better parcel than three keepsakes and nothing to use.

## The one thing worth saying out loud about the crib

In a house with a newborn, there is one class of gift that needs handling carefully, and it is the soft toy.

The guidance from the American Academy of Pediatrics, updated in 2022, is that a baby should always be placed on their back to sleep in a crib or bassinet that meets safety standards, and that the sleep space should contain nothing but a fitted sheet. Soft toys, pillows, blankets, and crib bumpers are not part of that space. Related to this, the Safe Sleep for Babies Act of 2021 banned both crib bumpers and inclined sleepers from sale in the United States.

So the rule for a first-Christmas gift is short. A soft toy can be lovely, and it can live on a shelf, in a pram, or in a photo, but it should not be the object left in the crib. If you are the one buying bedding for a newborn, buy a fitted sheet and nothing else. That is the whole caution, and it is not a reason to avoid toys. It is a reason to keep one category out of one place.

## Safe sleep in one paragraph

Three more facts are worth knowing if you are buying anything for a nursery. Room sharing, where the baby sleeps in the parents' room in their own bed, is recommended for ideally at least the first six months, and 90 percent of sleep-related infant deaths happen in that first half-year, which is why the sleep space matters so much so early. Around 3,500 infants a year in the United States die from sleep-related causes. And flat is the standard: a surface inclined more than ten degrees is not safe for infant sleep, which is why an inclined sleeper is not an alternative to a crib, and why nothing that claims to reduce the risk of SIDS should be treated as a guarantee.

## Gifts that earn their place by the end of winter

The best first-Christmas presents are the ones that solve a January problem rather than a December photo.

- A tummy time mat that is easy to wipe and rolls up small, so it can move between rooms.
- A stack of board books, because they survive a mouth and get read for years after that. A small set of [sturdy board books for a first library](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=board+books+for+babies&tag=cnxixi-20) is the least risky gift on this page.
- Something for the adults: food, an afternoon of help, a full night of sleep.
- A wearable blanket worn on the body, which is the standard substitute for a loose blanket in the crib.
- One keepsake that stays on a shelf, chosen deliberately rather than by default.

## If you are the grandparent

You are the person who can give the thing a parent will not buy for themselves, and that is usually time or a small luxury rather than another object. A promise to come and hold the baby for two hours on a Saturday in January lands better than a third soft toy, and it costs the household nothing in floor space.

If you want something in a box to hand over on the day, [a baby book the family can fill in during the first year](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=baby+memory+book+first+year&tag=cnxixi-20) is the sort of gift that is still on the shelf when the child is grown. It is also a gift that quietly asks the parents for something, which is why it should come with an offer to help write a bit of it.

## What to skip

- Anything with batteries, screens, or a claimed developmental benefit, because a baby of a few months will not use it for long enough for any of that to matter.
- A crib bumper set or an inclined sleeper, neither of which can legally be sold and neither of which belongs in a crib.
- Small stuffed animals bought specifically for the crib itself, however good the photo would be.
- Furniture or equipment the parents have not chosen, because they have already researched it more thoroughly than you have.

## The bottom line

The baby will not notice the present, so give one the parents will notice in January. Buy useful, buy in bulk, or buy time, and if you want something memorable, buy one keepsake and be clear that it is exactly one. The only safety point that needs remembering is the crib: a fitted sheet and nothing else, with the soft toy kept on the shelf rather than in the sleep space. After that, the whole thing is about the adults, and they are the ones who should be receiving it.
