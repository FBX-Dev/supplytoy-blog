A first birthday is less about the child than about the adults — a milestone for the parents, and an occasion that produces a genuinely difficult gift-buying problem. The child will not remember it, will not understand the wrapping, and will probably be more interested in the paper than in anything inside it.

Which means the correct question is not "what makes a good birthday present" but "what will still be in use in six months". Here are gifts that pass that test, organised by the situation you are actually in.

## Before anything else: the gift that is always correct

**Ask the parents what they need.** Not a failure of imagination — a one-year-old's household has a specific list of things they are short of, and they will tell you if asked.

If you would rather not ask, or the parents are too polite to say, the categories below are ordered by how reliably they get used.

## Gifts that get used for years

### A push-along wagon or walker

The most reliably loved gift at this age, and the one that lasts longest. A wagon supports cruising at twelve months, gets hauled around the house at two, filled with things at three, and used as a seat at four.

Look for a wide wheelbase, wheels with some friction, and a handle at the right height for a child who is not yet steady. Avoid anything with a seat that a child sits inside — the child needs their feet on the floor doing the work. See [push-along walkers and wagons](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=push+along+walker+wagon+baby&tag=cnxixi-20).

### A set of nesting cups or stacking cups

Almost free, endlessly used, survives everything, works in the bath, the sandpit and the car. There is no gift at any price with a better ratio of use to cost.

If you want it to feel like a proper present, buy two sets — one for home, one for the grandparents' house or the changing bag.

### A large, mixed-shape set of hardwood blocks

Twenty-five to thirty pieces, untreated or oiled rather than painted, with a real mix of shapes rather than all cubes.

At twelve months these are handled and banged. At two they are stacked. At four they build a house. This is a ten-year gift. Buy it now rather than at a later birthday.

### A low, open toy shelf

Not glamorous and quietly transformative. Toys on a low open shelf at a child's height get used; toys in a lidded chest do not. It is the least exciting item on this list and possibly the most useful.

If you want to pair it with something, put three small things on it.

### A child-sized chair and table

At twelve months this is somewhere to climb. From about eighteen months it is a workspace for drawing, puzzles and snack time. It changes how long a child stays with an activity.

Choose something stable enough to be leaned on and low enough that feet are flat on the floor.

## Gifts that are used every day

### Board books

Ten to fifteen thick-paged board books with high contrast, faces, a lift-the-flap and a rhyme. Rounded corners, chunky pages, small enough for a one-year-old to hold.

If you want to make it feel like a gift, put them in a low basket that the child can reach — the basket is part of the present.

### A push-along or walker wagon, again

Worth listing twice because it is genuinely the best large gift at this age. If someone else has already bought one, move to the next section.

### Bath toys that will not grow mould

Open-bottomed cups, open-hulled boats, waterproof books, pouring tools. Nothing that squeaks, nothing with batteries.

Bath toys are usually a household's most worn-out category, and a set of cups and pouring tools is both cheap and genuinely better than what it replaces. See [bath toys without holes](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bath+toys+cups+open&tag=cnxixi-20).

### A floor mirror

Unbreakable, freestanding or cot-mounted. Babies find their own reflection absorbing for months, and it supports tummy time into the bargain. Unusual as a gift, and consistently well used.

### A photo album of familiar faces

A small board photo book filled with pictures of the people the child sees regularly. Grandparents, siblings, pets. Babies in this age range are intensely interested in faces, and a book of their own family is the most-read book in many houses.

This is the gift people remember you for, and it costs almost nothing.

## Gifts for the child who has everything

When the house is already full, buy consumables or experiences rather than objects.

- **Art supplies.** Chunky crayons, washable markers, a large pad of paper. Consumed and replaced, so never clutter.
- **A roll of paper** and a roll of low-tack tape. Genuinely used, genuinely disposable.
- **A membership** to a local zoo, aquarium, farm park or soft-play centre.
- **A contribution to a savings account.** Unglamorous, and the parents will be more grateful in eighteen years than they can express now.
- **A day out with you.** Especially valuable for grandparents: an afternoon as a present, with no object to store.
- **A box of clothes in the next size up.** The one thing every household is always short of.

## What to avoid

**Anything with a screen.** A one-year-old does not need a screen, and a screen gift is a gift with an agenda attached that the parents may not share.

**Very large plush toys.** A three-foot teddy is a memorable photograph and a permanent storage problem. A child at twelve months cannot carry it, so it becomes an ornament.

**Toys that require batteries and make noise.** The noise is a gift to the child and a cost to the parents, and the parents will be the ones paying it.

**Toys with small parts, regardless of what the box says.** If a part fits inside a toilet roll tube, a twelve-month-old will find it.

**Very specific hobby or interest items.** A train set or a doll's house is a gift for a four-year-old. Buying two years early means two years of storage.

**Gift sets with twenty components.** The parts get separated and the whole thing becomes unmanageable.

## If you want to buy clothes

The practical rules: buy the size labelled twelve to eighteen months or eighteen to twenty-four months, never the size the child currently wears, because the child will grow out of it before they wear it. Buy washable, buy things that open flat for changing, and buy one nice outfit plus several practical everyday items rather than several nice outfits.

## A word on the party itself

If you are the parent: the first birthday is for you, and it is entirely reasonable to keep it small. A one-year-old will be overwhelmed by a room full of adults and will need a nap partway through.

The photographs will be better, and the child will be happier, with six people and a cake than with forty and a schedule.

## The bottom line

Buy a push-along walker or wagon, a set of nesting cups, a mixed-shape wooden block set and a low open shelf — or, if the house is already full, buy art supplies, a photo album of familiar faces, or an afternoon out.

Whatever you choose, check it against the one question that matters: will this still be in use next summer?
