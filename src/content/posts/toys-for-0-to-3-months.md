The newborn section of a toy shop is the largest shelf for the smallest customer, and very little on it is badly made. It fails for a different reason: a two-month-old cannot use most of it. The activity gym with four hanging toys, the projector that plays lullabies, the rattle designed to be grasped, the wall of products that promise to help a baby sleep — almost all of them are waiting for a skill that arrives later, and in the meantime they take up floor space and get in the way of the few things that do work.

This is the age band where the most money is spent and the least is needed. Knowing what actually does the work is most of the saving.

## The crib is bare, and that is the specification

The American Academy of Pediatrics updated its recommendations on sleep-related infant deaths in 2022, and the sentence that shapes every purchase for this age is blunt: the crib should be bare. A fitted sheet is the only thing in it.

That means no pillow, no pillow-like toy, no comforter, no quilt, no mattress topper, no crib bumper, no plush animal and no loose bedding, not even arranged neatly around the edges. A baby should be placed on their back to sleep, on a surface that is flat and not inclined, in a crib or bassinet that meets the current federal standard, and left there without decoration.

None of this is fussiness. Every item on that list is a thing a newborn can end up face down against, and a newborn cannot roll back.

## Why this age band gets the strictest rules

The 2024 injury figures explain why the guidance for the first year is written in harder language than the guidance for older children. In the Consumer Product Safety Commission's 2024 report, children aged four and under had the highest toy-related emergency department injury rate of any age group, at 497 per 100,000. For comparison, the rate across all ages was 79, and for the 14 and under group it was 306.

That is a striking result for the group least able to move: 34 per cent of all toy-related emergency department injuries that year were in children four and under. Head and face injuries accounted for 45 per cent of the total, and 19 per cent were lacerations.

The reading for a parent of a newborn is not that toys are dangerous. It is that the smallest children absorb the highest rate of harm, so the bar for what goes into their space should be set by evidence rather than by what a shelf is selling.

## The number that should shape the whole shopping list

About 3,500 infants die each year in the United States from sleep-related causes, and roughly 90 per cent of sudden infant deaths and accidental deaths happen in the first six months of life. That is why the sleep environment, rather than the toy box, is where the first three months are won or lost.

The single change with the clearest effect is to share a room without sharing a bed. Room-sharing, with the baby in their own crib or bassinet in the parents' room, can reduce the risk of SIDS by up to 50 per cent, and the recommendation runs to at least six months. Feeding and settling can happen anywhere, but the baby should go back into a separate sleep space afterwards, ideally in the parents' room.

The reasons to be careful with bed-sharing are specific rather than general. Sharing a sleep surface on a sofa, a soft armchair or with cushions carries a risk up to 67 times higher; sharing with someone who is exhausted, has taken sedating medication or alcohol, or smokes raises it about tenfold; and being under four months old, sleeping with someone who is not a parent, or being born preterm or at low birth weight raises it five to ten times. A newborn ticks several of those boxes simply by being a newborn.

## What actually works in the first three months

Strip away the marketing and the first three months have a very short list of things that reliably do something.

The list is a face at close range, a voice that talks directly to the baby, and time on the floor. A newborn's usable vision is measured in inches, so a parent's face is the highest-contrast object in the room. Being talked to, imitated and answered is the beginning of language, and it is free. Lying on a flat surface with the freedom to kick is the activity that builds the strength for everything that comes next.

Almost everything sold for this age is an attempt to package one of those three, and the packaging is usually worse than the original.

## Tummy time, the one structured activity worth scheduling

Tummy time is the only item on the newborn calendar that behaves like a task, and the guidance on it is more concrete than most parents expect. It should be done while the baby is awake and supervised, it can begin shortly after coming home from hospital, it should be short and frequent, and the aim is to build up to at least 15 to 30 minutes in total each day by seven weeks of age.

The purpose is twofold. It develops the neck, shoulder and trunk strength that precedes rolling and sitting, and it reduces the chance of positional plagiocephaly, the flattening of one side of the back of the head that happens when a baby spends too long in one position. A [firm, flat play mat](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=baby+play+mat+firm+for+tummy+time&tag=cnxixi-20) is the only piece of equipment it needs.

The practical version is to fold it into things you already do rather than booking a session. A minute or two on a parent's chest counts, so does a minute after each nappy change. A baby who objects loudly should be picked up and tried again later; the total matters more than any one attempt.

## High-contrast patterns, the one purchase worth making

There is a reason black and white cards and books are the first things that hold a newborn's attention. At this age the visual system resolves strong contrast far better than it resolves colour, so a bold geometric pattern is genuinely more interesting to a baby than a pastel illustration.

This is the category where a small amount of money buys something real. [A set of high-contrast black and white cards](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=high+contrast+black+and+white+cards+for+babies&tag=cnxixi-20) can be propped beside the changing mat, held up during a wake window, or folded into the nappy bag, and it stays useful for months. Look for simple, large shapes rather than busy detail, and choose something that can be wiped clean.

What matters is that the card is held where the baby can actually see it, roughly a forearm's length away, and that it is moved slowly. A card propped at the far end of a cot is a decoration.

## The mattress and the sheet

If there is one purchase worth being deliberate about, it is the surface the baby sleeps on. It should be firm rather than soft, so that the baby's face does not form a hollow in it, and it should be flat, because a baby placed on an incline can slide into a position where the chin drops towards the chest.

A [firm crib mattress with a well-fitted sheet](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=crib+mattress+firm+with+fitted+sheet&tag=cnxixi-20) is the whole of the sleeping setup. The sheet needs to fit tightly enough that it cannot be pulled loose at a corner, and it should be changed rather than layered, because a stack of sheets is a soft surface.

## Sleep products to walk past

The equipment marketed for infant sleep is where most of the money and most of the risk sits, so it is worth being direct about it.

- No consumer device has been shown to reduce the risk of SIDS, and the academy does not recommend home cardiorespiratory monitors for healthy babies. Anything claiming to prevent SIDS is selling reassurance rather than protection, which is its own kind of harm.
- Cot bumpers and inclined sleepers are banned from manufacture and sale in the United States under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act of 2021. This is a legal prohibition rather than a preference.
- Sitting devices are not sleep spaces. Car seats, strollers, swings, carriers and slings are not recommended for routine sleep, and the caution is strongest for babies under four months old.
- Swaddling should stop once a baby shows signs of starting to roll, which typically happens around three months.
- A pacifier offered at nap and night sleep is one of the few items with a positive recommendation attached, and it is worth knowing that before the first hard night.

## What we would not buy

- Any product advertised as reducing the risk of SIDS, including breathing monitors for a healthy baby.
- Crib bumpers, padded liners, cot nests, pillows or positioners of any kind.
- A soft mattress, a mattress topper or a thick quilt.
- Hanging toys that clip inside the cot rather than above the reach of a lying baby.
- An activity gym bought for a two-week-old; the interest arrives later and the resale value does not.
- More than a couple of rattles, since a newborn cannot yet hold one on purpose.

## The bottom line

For the first three months the crib holds a fitted sheet and nothing else, the baby sleeps on their back on a flat, firm surface in the parents' room in their own space, and the toys that matter are a face, a voice and a floor. Buy a firm mattress with a tight sheet, a flat play mat, and a set of high-contrast cards. Build tummy time up to 15 to 30 minutes a day by seven weeks. Skip anything that claims to reduce the risk of SIDS, and skip the bumpers, pillows and positioners altogether — they are banned for a reason.
